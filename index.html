    How is this worked out?

    Why it matters. You forget what you don't go back to. Leave a topic too long and you're not revising it any more, you're relearning it. Pile up enough of those and you end up cramming the night before, stressed, and losing marks on questions you once could do.

    When a topic is due. Red topics need you back after 3 days, Amber after 7, Green after 14. Shakier topics fade faster, so they come round sooner. A topic you've never revised is always due.

    What comes first. The longer a topic has been overdue, and the weaker it is, the higher it ranks. Red counts 3×, Amber 2×, Green 1×.

    No module left behind. If a module hasn't had its weekly minimum yet (weeks start Monday), its topics get pushed up the list, even ones that aren't due yet.

    How does the simulation work?

    It copies your habits. It looks at how often you've studied each module in the last 4 weeks and keeps you at that pace, with some random busy days and lazy days thrown in.

    You get better when you revise. A session sometimes moves a topic up a level (Red to Amber, Amber to Green). It doesn't always, just like real revision.

    You get worse when you don't. Leave a topic for twice its due gap and it starts slipping back down a level.

    "Days overdue" adds up how late every topic is. 3 topics each 5 days late = 15 days overdue. It's a rough measure of how much relearning is piling up.

    The blue line is the same you, except you always hit each module's weekly minimum. Nothing else changes, so the gap between the lines is what those minimums are worth.

    It's a rough model to motivate you, not a crystal ball. The more sessions you log, the better it knows you.
